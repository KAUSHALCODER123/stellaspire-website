CFO Hiring Process: A Step-by-Step Guide

From defining the mandate to the first 90 days. Most delays come from an unclear mandate, not a lack of candidates.

By Nikita Agrawal, Founder & CEOUpdated 9 October 20266 min read

Key takeaways

  • Hire a CFO when finance becomes strategic: fundraising, rapid scale or a major transaction.
  • Agree the mandate and the decision-makers before you write the job description.
  • The best CFO candidates are rarely looking, so plan for direct, discreet approaches.
  • Assess judgment, stakeholder leadership and team building, not just technical finance.
  • Agree a 90-day plan before the CFO joins.

When does a company need a CFO?

The moment usually arrives when finance stops being a reporting function and becomes a strategic one. Common triggers:

  • Fundraising or an IPO path, where investors expect a finance leader who owns the equity story and the data room.
  • Rapid scale, when budgeting, cash planning and unit economics need one accountable owner.
  • Regulatory complexity, such as becoming an NBFC or entering new markets.
  • A GCC setup, where an India finance leader must work closely with a global CFO.
  • Mergers, acquisitions or restructuring, which demand deal experience and board communication.

If none of these apply yet, a senior financial controller or head of finance may be the better hire.

The CFO hiring process, step by step

  1. Agree why you are hiring, and who decides

    Align founders, CEO and board on the business problem the CFO will solve, and name the interview panel at the start.

  2. Define the mandate

    Write down what the CFO must achieve in the first 12 to 24 months, and separate must-haves from nice-to-haves.

  3. Write the job description

    Turn the mandate into a document that sells the opportunity as well as listing requirements.

  4. Choose the search approach

    In-house, your network, or a specialist partner. Confidential or senior searches are usually retained; see retained vs contingency search.

  5. Map and approach the market

    The best candidates are rarely looking. Map relevant companies and adjacent pools, then approach people directly and discreetly.

  6. Assess against the mandate

    Structured interviews, a business case or presentation, and thorough referencing.

  7. Close the offer and plan onboarding

    Handle fixed pay, variable pay, incentives and notice periods carefully, and agree a 90-day plan before the CFO joins.

Tip

Write the mandate on one page and get every decision-maker to sign it. Use the same page to brief interviewers and to test each shortlisted candidate.

Writing the CFO job description

Senior candidates judge the company by how clearly it describes the role. Include:

  • The context: stage, ownership, funding position and why the role exists now.
  • The mandate: the three to five outcomes the CFO will own in the first two years.
  • Scope and reporting: reporting line, board exposure and the functions covered.
  • Must-have experience: qualifications and sector or transaction experience where they genuinely matter.
  • What makes it attractive: growth, ownership, impact and the leadership team.

How to assess CFO candidates

Technical finance is the entry ticket. What separates good CFOs from great ones is judgment, communication and team building. Assess:

  • Strategic finance: can they link financial decisions to strategy, pricing and capital allocation?
  • Stakeholder leadership: the board, investors, auditors and, in a GCC, the global parent.
  • Team and systems: have they built finance teams and controls at your stage?
  • Integrity and judgment: how they handled pressure and disagreements with leadership.
  • Fit with the CEO: plan informal conversations as well as formal interviews.

Tip

Give each interviewer one or two areas to score, ask for evidence rather than impressions, and compare notes in one debrief.

Common CFO hiring mistakes

  • Hiring for the last stage, not the next one.
  • Too many decision-makers, too late. Bring the board in at the mandate stage.
  • Unrealistic pay expectations. Test the budget against the market before the shortlist.
  • A narrow shortlist. Search actively for qualified women finance leaders and adjacent sectors; our diversity hiring approach builds this in.
  • No onboarding plan. The first 90 days shape how the board sees the new CFO.

A first-90-days plan for a new CFO

  • Days 1 to 30: listen and learn. Meet the board, leadership team, finance team, auditors, bankers and key investors. Review the numbers, cash position and open audit points.
  • Days 31 to 60: diagnose and prioritise. Share an honest view of people, processes, systems and controls with the CEO, and agree the few priorities that link to the mandate.
  • Days 61 to 90: act and report. Deliver early improvements and present a plan for the rest of the year to the board or the global parent.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to hire a CFO?

It depends on the clarity of the mandate, the talent pool and the candidate's notice period, so plan the timeline with notice periods in mind.

Should a startup hire a CFO or a finance controller?

A controller or head of finance may be enough for accurate books and compliance; a CFO makes sense when fundraising, investor relations or a major transaction is on the horizon.

What qualifications should a CFO have?

Many CFOs in India are chartered accountants, and some hold an MBA, CPA or ACCA, but leadership experience and transaction exposure usually matter more.

Who should be on the CFO interview panel?

Usually the CEO, a board member or investor, and a senior peer, plus the global CFO for a GCC role, each with a clear area to assess.
