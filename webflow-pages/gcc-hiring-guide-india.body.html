GCC Hiring in India: The Complete 2026 Guide

Setting up or scaling a Global Capability Centre is, above all, a hiring project. The phases, the roles that matter, the hub cities and the mistakes that slow new centres down.

Key takeaways

  • Hire the site leader and first function heads before the wider team. They shape culture and attract the next hires.
  • Plan hiring in three phases: founding leadership, core functional teams, then scale.
  • Benchmark pay against the local GCC market for the role and city, not the parent country.
  • Build gender balance from the first hire. It is much harder to correct later.
  • Agree early with the parent who owns each hiring decision.

What is a GCC?

A Global Capability Centre is an offshore unit owned by a global company, not an outsourcing vendor. It delivers finance, analytics, technology and operations, and increasingly owns end-to-end processes and products.

That shift from support centre to capability centre changes who you hire: leaders who can own outcomes, influence global stakeholders and build teams.

The three hiring phases of a GCC

  1. Founding leadership

    A site head or centre director, plus the leaders of the first functions to move. These searches are usually confidential and best run as retained searches, for people who have built a centre before and are credible with global leadership.

  2. Core functional teams

    Managers and senior individual contributors who will run processes and transitions: FP&A managers, record-to-report leads, data engineers, BI leads. Transition and documentation experience matters as much as technical skill.

  3. Scale and new capabilities

    Hiring becomes more repeatable and volumes rise, while mature centres add decision science, AI and finance transformation. Many companies mix in-house recruiters and specialist partners here.

Tip

Involve your founding leaders in hiring their own direct reports. Candidates often join for the person they will work for.

The first ten hires in a new GCC

Each hire should make the next one easier. A reasonable order:

  1. Site head or centre director. Owns the centre and represents it to the parent.
  2. HR or talent acquisition lead. Runs hiring, onboarding and local policies.
  3. Head of the first function to move. A controller, head of finance operations or analytics lead.
  4. Finance and admin support for the entity. Payroll, compliance and vendor set-up.
  5. First-line managers in the lead function. The people who receive the transition.
  6. Senior individual contributors. Experts who document processes and train later hires.
  7. Head of the second function. Once the first is stable.
  8. Technology or systems lead. Understands the parent's tools and data controls.
  9. Transition or programme manager. Tracks knowledge transfer.
  10. Early analysts and associates. Hired once managers are in place to supervise them.

Choosing a hub city

  • Bengaluru. The deepest pool for technology, data, AI and GCC leaders, and the most competition.
  • Hyderabad. Technology, pharma and finance operations, with strong campus infrastructure.
  • Pune. Engineering, automotive and manufacturing-linked centres, and a growing finance base.
  • Chennai. Engineering, banking operations, and finance and accounting.
  • Delhi NCR. Finance, consulting and business services talent, close to head offices.
  • Mumbai. Banking, financial services, risk and treasury.

Common GCC hiring mistakes

  1. Hiring the team before the leader.
  2. Benchmarking pay against the wrong market. GCC talent compares offers with other GCCs.
  3. Underestimating notice periods. Ask at the first screening call, not at offer stage.
  4. Treating diversity as a later phase.
  5. Assessing only technical skill. Stakeholder management across time zones matters as much.

Frequently asked questions

What does GCC stand for?

Global Capability Centre: an offshore unit owned by a global company that delivers finance, analytics, technology and operations for the parent.

Who should a new GCC hire first?

The site head and the leaders of the first functions to move, because they shape the culture and attract the next hires.

How is GCC hiring different from IT staffing?

GCC hiring builds permanent teams that belong to the parent company, so fit, leadership and retention matter; IT staffing supplies contract resources for projects.

Which city is best for a new GCC in India?

It depends on the talent you need most: Bengaluru for technology, data and AI, Mumbai for banking and treasury, with Hyderabad, Pune, Chennai and Delhi NCR each strong in their own areas.
