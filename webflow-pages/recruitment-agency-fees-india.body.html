How Much Does a Recruitment Agency Charge in India? (2026 Guide)

Fees are usually a percentage of the hire's annual pay. The model, the seniority of the role and the terms behind the fee matter as much as the number.

By Nikita Agrawal, Founder & CEO Updated 9 October 2026 5 min read

Key takeaways

  • Most agencies in India charge a one-time fee, a percentage of the hire's annual CTC.
  • Contingency fees are paid when the candidate joins. Retained search fees are paid in instalments.
  • Fees rise with seniority, scarcity and confidentiality.
  • Check what the fee is calculated on: fixed CTC, or total CTC with variable pay.
  • Replacement terms and payment milestones matter as much as the rate.

The three pricing models

Compare models before you compare numbers. Two quotes with the same percentage can buy very different levels of commitment.

  1. Contingency recruitment

    You pay only if a candidate the agency introduces joins, usually on the joining date and calculated on annual fixed CTC. It suits specialist and mid-level roles. The trade-off is that agencies paid only on success prioritise roles they can close quickly.

  2. Retained (executive) search

    For CFOs, finance heads and GCC site leaders, the fee is paid in stages: at the start, at shortlist and at joining. In return you get an exclusive, research-led search with a market map. See retained vs contingency search for when each fits.

  3. Dedicated hiring and RPO

    For several roles at once, agencies offer a dedicated recruiter, a project fee or a monthly retainer. These trade a lower per-hire cost for a longer commitment.

What percentage is typical in India?

There is no official rate card. These are the ranges most commonly quoted:

Junior and volume rolesContingency or bulk pricingOften around one month's salary (about 8.33% of annual CTC) or a flat fee
Mid-level specialist rolesContingencyCommonly in the region of 8.33% to 15% of annual fixed CTC
Senior specialist and niche rolesContingency or exclusiveCommonly 15% to 20% of annual fixed CTC

Treat these as orientation, not benchmarks. What matters is what the fee includes.

What drives the price of a search

  • Seniority. Senior roles take longer and carry a higher cost of a wrong hire.
  • Scarcity. Niche profiles take more research to find.
  • Confidentiality. Replacing an incumbent needs discreet, direct approaches.
  • Exclusivity and volume. An exclusive mandate or several hires for one team can change the rate.
  • Replacement terms. A longer guarantee carries more risk for the agency and is priced in.

Terms to check before you sign

What the percentage is calculated on

Fixed CTC only, or total CTC including variable pay, joining bonuses and benefits. The difference is significant for senior roles.

Tip

Ask the agency to work the fee out on a sample offer for your role, in writing.

Replacement guarantee

Confirm the length of the period, whether it covers resignations and terminations, and whether it is a replacement or a partial refund.

Payment schedule and GST

Contingency fees are due on joining; retained fees are split across milestones. Fees are generally quoted exclusive of GST.

Candidate ownership and cancellation

Check how long the agency is owed a fee for a candidate it introduced, and what happens to retained instalments if you cancel or fill the role internally.

Red flags in a recruitment agreement

  • A vague fee basis. Ask for the exact calculation in writing.
  • Open-ended candidate ownership. A claim with no time limit leads to disputes.
  • No replacement terms. If they are not spelled out, assume you will not get one.
  • A fee due before joining, outside retained search.
  • Any fee charged to candidates. A legitimate agency is paid by the hiring company.

Questions to ask before you sign

Frequently asked questions

Do candidates pay recruitment agencies in India?

No. Legitimate agencies are paid by the hiring company, never by candidates.

When is a recruitment fee paid?

Under contingency, when the candidate joins. Under retained search, in instalments at the start, at shortlist and at joining.

Is the fee calculated on CTC or fixed salary?

It depends on the agreement. Many agencies use annual fixed CTC; some use total CTC, so confirm the basis in writing.
